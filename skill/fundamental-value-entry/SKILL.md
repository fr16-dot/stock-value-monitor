# Fundamental Value + Momentum Entry

## Purpose

Produce two independent, compatible decisions:

1. **Valuation signal:** calculate intrinsic value and fixed entry zones without using the current share price.
2. **Momentum signal:** determine whether a verified catalyst and market confirmation justify a limited right-side entry, even when valuation is fair or expensive.

Apply these principles:

> Value anchors long-term return and maximum size. Momentum determines whether and how to enter before value catches up. Price action may change the momentum signal; it may not silently change intrinsic value.

Avoid the impossible loop of waiting while a high-quality stock is reasonably priced, then refusing to buy after confirmation because it rose.

## Required behavior

1. Use current primary sources: filings, investor releases, presentations, calls, official guidance, and industry data.
2. Establish price-blind bear/base/bull assumptions before retrieving the current price or designing technical execution levels.
3. Separate sourced facts, analytical assumptions, and market-price observations.
4. Cite current financial data and material external facts. Use at least two suitable valuation methods when practical; never manufacture precision.
5. Preserve fixed valuation zones until fundamentals, estimates, capital structure, competitive position, risk, or justified multiples materially change.
6. Treat catalyst/momentum levels as event-specific and temporary. Never relabel them as fair value.
7. Give exact buy-now, pullback, no-pullback, confirmation, and exit actions as percentages of the target position.
8. Make a meaningful initial purchase when either a high-quality stock is in a valuation buy zone or a reflexive stock has a chase-qualified catalyst.
9. State both signals even when they conflict, such as “valuation expensive; momentum chase-qualified.”
10. Highlight downside, dilution, leverage, and evidence that would invalidate each sleeve.
11. Express uncertainty through tranche size, target weight, and failure conditions. Do not turn ordinary earnings or tape uncertainty into a zero-position recommendation for an A/B business already in a valid buy zone.
12. Scan cross-sectionally when reviewing a watchlist. A cluster of simultaneous opportunities requires a portfolio-level deployment decision, not isolated ticker notes.
13. Treat a missing or stale valuation record as work to complete, not a reason to suppress an alert. Create a price-blind provisional record and label its confidence.
14. Track “recommended” and “user-confirmed executed” separately. Never infer that a suggested trade became a holding.
15. For every active watchlist ticker, require exactly one ACTIVE valuation or an explicit non-corporate monitoring model. Run the completeness audit before declaring that no trigger exists.
16. Never map an unvalued security to `ABOVE_RB`, `RB`, `HV`, or `EV`. Use `UNVALUED`/`NO_ZONE` until a valid record exists, and preserve the alert obligation.
17. When a user adds a ticker to a watchlist, create a price-blind provisional valuation in the same workflow, including for EXTENDED names. Do not defer valuation until the name becomes a candidate.
18. Let a material guidance, licensing, financing, order, regulatory, or capital-structure event bypass EXTENDED-tier lightweight filtering and force a full fundamental and momentum review.
19. Validate quote freshness against the security's exchange and session. A stale, delayed, or wrong-exchange quote may be retained for reference but cannot drive zone mapping, trigger suppression, or a “no action” conclusion.
20. Resolve ticker identity before research or valuation: verify company legal name, exchange, currency, security type, and—when available—CIK/issuer identifier. A reused ticker, merger, rebrand, ADR-ratio change, or listing transfer invalidates inherited company facts and requires an identity-change review.

For watchlist monitors, missed-opportunity audits, or system repairs, read [references/lessen_learnrd.md](references/lessen_learnrd.md) and apply its incident-derived controls.

## Step 1 — Define the decision

Identify:

- Ticker and company.
- Initiating, adding, holding, reducing, or trading around a core.
- Holding period; default to 3–5 years for the value sleeve and several weeks to two quarters for the momentum sleeve.
- Existing weight, target weight, and portfolio role when known.

Do not delay for missing portfolio details. Give target-position percentages and translate them into an illustrative portfolio weight.

## Step 2 — Route the stock

Assign one route before valuation:

- **V — Valuation-led:** durable cash flows and estimates; price mainly follows earnings and multiples.
- **H — Hybrid/reflexive:** expectations, narrative, optionality, positioning, and estimate revisions can move faster than conventional fair value. Examples include hypergrowth AI software, autonomous-driving platforms, emerging infrastructure, and other winner-take-most stories.
- **T — Tape-only/speculative:** price is moving without a verified fundamental catalyst or adequate disclosure.

Use the valuation workflow for every route. Add the momentum workflow for H and for event-driven V stocks. Classify T stocks as no chase under this skill; do not manufacture a fundamental justification from price action.

## Step 3 — Gather fundamentals

Review at minimum:

- Revenue and organic/segment growth; sequential acceleration.
- Gross, operating, net, and incremental margins.
- GAAP and adjusted EPS, including reconciliation quality.
- Operating cash flow, capex, FCF, and FCF margin.
- Cash, debt, net cash/debt, liquidity, and maturities.
- Diluted shares, stock compensation, issuance, repurchases, and per-share growth.
- Guidance, consensus expectations, and the mathematical growth implied by guidance.
- ARR, RPO, backlog, TCV, NRR, bookings, utilization, pricing, capacity, deliveries, or other relevant leading indicators.
- Customer concentration, cyclicality, regulation, execution, and competitive threats.

For narrative/optionality businesses, identify measurable milestones that convert the story into cash flow: paid deployments, usage, contracted capacity, regulatory approval, unit economics, production scale, take rates, or verifiable customer adoption.

Use pre-event consensus only to measure a reported surprise. Defer current post-event consensus, analyst targets, and market-implied multiples until after the price-blind forecast is locked.

## Step 4 — Grade business quality

Assign one grade:

- **A:** durable compounder with a strong moat, balance sheet, cash generation, and visibility.
- **B:** high quality with meaningful cyclicality, concentration, execution, or valuation sensitivity.
- **C:** attractive growth but limited visibility, weak profitability, leverage, or dilution risk.
- **D:** thesis is primarily speculative or insufficiently validated.

Explain revenue durability, pricing power, moat, reinvestment runway, ROIC, balance-sheet resilience, and capital allocation. The grade controls maximum size, not whether a genuine momentum event exists.

## Step 5 — Evaluate the latest report or catalyst

Determine:

1. Growth acceleration versus prior quarters and prior guidance.
2. Beat quality: core demand, price, acquisition, accounting, or one-time item.
3. Incremental margin and cash-conversion direction.
4. EPS quality, taxes, investment gains, buybacks, and stock compensation.
5. Guidance change in dollars and percentage points, not merely “raised.”
6. Leading-indicator confirmation.
7. Whether the event expands duration, probability, or terminal economics.

Classify the fundamental thesis as materially strengthened, modestly strengthened, unchanged, modestly weakened, or materially impaired.

## Step 6 — Build a price-blind forecast

Before looking at the current price, lock independent bear/base/bull assumptions for the next 1–3 years:

- Revenue and key operating drivers.
- Margins, EPS, and FCF.
- Share count and dilution.
- Net cash/debt and capital intensity.
- Terminal or exit multiple.
- Scenario probabilities.

Normalize cyclical earnings. For optionality businesses, probability-weight major future businesses instead of assigning full value to every narrative.

Ask which multiple is justified by quality, duration, growth, cyclicality, rates, and peer/history evidence. Never ask which multiple produces a value above the market price.

## Step 7 — Select valuation methods

Use at least two suitable methods when practical:

- Mature profitable: normalized forward P/E, EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF, or DCF.
- High-growth software: EV/revenue with plausible steady-state margins, EV/FCF, and Rule of 40/retention checks.
- Semiconductors/hardware: mid-cycle P/E, EV/EBITDA, and normalized pricing/capacity.
- Unprofitable growth: scenario EV/revenue, cash runway, dilution, and time to breakeven.
- Power/commodity: normalized prices, EBITDA, FCF, leverage, contracts, and asset value.
- Platform/optionality: sum of parts, probability-weighted real options, and reverse DCF/expectations testing.

Use reverse DCF only to show what the market must believe. Do not call those embedded expectations fair value.

## Step 8 — Calculate intrinsic value and fixed zones

Report bear, base, bull, probability-weighted value, 12–24 month expected return, and approximate 3-year base-case annualized return.

Derive fixed zones from base value, bear downside, quality, and required return:

- **Extremely attractive:** usually 18%+ base-case 3-year annual return; buy 70%–100% of target.
- **High-value buy:** usually 15%+; buy 50%–70%.
- **Reasonable buy:** usually 10%–15%; buy 30%–50%.
- **Fair hold:** positive but inadequate margin of safety; hold or initiate slowly.
- **Overvalued:** requires the bull case or offers inadequate long-term return; pause value-sleeve additions.

Do not change these zones because price moved. Change them only through the valuation change log.

Do not define fair value from the analyst target-price average. For an A-grade company in the reasonable-buy zone, do not remain entirely uninvested solely because a 5%–10% pullback may occur.

Apply these minimum action floors as percentages of the final target position when fundamentals are intact:

| Grade | Reasonable buy | High-value buy | Extremely attractive |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 30%–40% | 50%–70% | 70%–90% |
| B | 25%–35% | 40%–60% | 60%–80% |
| C | 5%–10% | 10%–20% | 20%–30% |
| D | 0% | 0%–5% speculative | 5%–10% speculative |

For C/D businesses, apply the floor only when liquidity, financing runway, dilution, and a defined failure point are acceptable. A report due within two trading sessions normally halves the A/B initial tranche; it does not reduce it to zero. Zero is appropriate only for a stale or invalid thesis, unresolved existential/accounting/capital-structure risk, unreliable price/liquidity, or a documented portfolio risk-limit breach. State which exception applies.

## Step 8A — Detect portfolio-wide opportunity clusters

For a watchlist, scheduled monitor, or “what should I buy?” request, read [references/watchlist-monitoring-framework.md](references/watchlist-monitoring-framework.md).

Run a full-list rescan when either condition holds:

- At least three A/B companies are simultaneously in reasonable-buy or deeper zones.
- At least five watchlist names fall 5% or more in one session and the dominant cause is macro/sector-wide rather than five independent thesis breaks.

Rank the top opportunities by business quality, valuation confidence, base upside, bear downside, balance-sheet resilience, and portfolio correlation—not by percentage decline. Issue one portfolio-level deployment plan in addition to ticker actions. Do not let per-ticker duplicate suppression hide a newly formed cluster.

## Step 9 — Retrieve current price and event tape

Only after Step 8, retrieve:

- Current price and timestamp.
- Prior close, event open, high, low, and close when available.
- Gap midpoint and whether the gap remains open.
- Relative volume versus a normal lookback.
- Market-relative and sector-relative strength.
- Short interest/options positioning only when reliable and relevant.

Label intraday observations as provisional. Do not claim a strong close before the session ends.

Read [references/momentum-entry-framework.md](references/momentum-entry-framework.md) whenever the user asks whether to chase, buy a gap, follow a breakout, or analyze a V/H stock after a major catalyst.

## Step 10 — Test for fundamental overselling

Classify a selloff as fundamentally oversold only when most are true:

- The price decline materially exceeds the reduction in intrinsic value.
- Core revenue, earnings, guidance, and competitive advantage remain substantially intact.
- Balance-sheet and liquidity risks remain acceptable.
- FCF is not structurally impaired.
- The concern is temporary, cyclical, capacity-related, sentiment-driven, or reversible.
- Price is in the reasonable-buy zone or better and bear downside is acceptable relative to upside.

Use RSI, moving averages, support, gaps, and volume only to time tranches. Never use them to set fair value or independently justify a value-sleeve purchase.

## Step 11 — Qualify the momentum event

Require all of the following for **chase-qualified** status:

1. A verified catalyst, not rumor or price action alone.
2. A material improvement in forward revenue, margins, FCF, contracted demand, or probability of a major business outcome.
3. Confirmation through strong relative price action and liquidity; normally elevated volume and price holding above the event open or gap midpoint.
4. A predefined event-failure level and acceptable position-level loss.
5. No new balance-sheet, dilution, regulatory, or accounting problem that invalidates the catalyst.

Classify the momentum signal:

- **Chase-qualified:** all gates pass; initiate 20%–30% of target for A/B businesses, or 10%–15% for C/D businesses.
- **Conditional starter:** fundamentals pass but tape or durability is unconfirmed; initiate 10%–15%.
- **Wait:** catalyst is good but price action fails confirmation or risk cannot be defined.
- **No chase:** move is price-only, rumor-driven, low-liquidity, or fundamentally contradicted.
- **Exit momentum sleeve:** event gap fails, estimates reverse, or catalyst is invalidated.

A 20%–30% gap is not automatically too late. A smaller gap is not automatically safe. Judge the magnitude and durability of the revision, the quality of the close, and defined risk.

## Step 12 — Combine valuation and momentum signals

Use this default matrix:

| Valuation signal | Momentum signal | Default action |
|---|---|---|
| Reasonable or better | Chase-qualified | Buy 40%–60% of target; both sleeves agree |
| Reasonable or better | Wait/no chase | Buy 30%–50% value sleeve; slow execution |
| Fair/overvalued | Chase-qualified | Buy 20%–30% momentum sleeve; cap at 50%–60% of target until another fundamental confirmation |
| Fair/overvalued | Wait/no chase | Do not initiate; hold existing core if thesis remains intact |

Adjust for grade and concentration. Default maximum target weights when portfolio context is absent:

- A: 4%–6%.
- B: 2.5%–4%.
- C: 1%–2%.
- D: 0%–0.5% speculative only.

These are starting guidelines, not guarantees or personalized risk limits.

## Step 13 — Build both sleeves

### Value sleeve

- Use fixed valuation zones.
- Size by expected return and downside.
- Use thesis-based stop-adding/reduction conditions rather than a tight mechanical stop.
- Provide a no-pullback completion path for reasonable/high-value A/B companies.

### Momentum sleeve

- State the exact initial percentage bought now.
- Add 15%–25% on event confirmation, a high-quality low-volume pullback, or a new closing high while fundamentals remain intact.
- Never require a pullback as the only completion path.
- Never average down solely because price fell.
- Use an event-based risk level, normally a decisive close below the event low, failed gap, broken event open, or reversed estimate trend.
- Reduce or exit the momentum sleeve when the event fails even if the long-term value thesis survives.

Keep sleeve accounting explicit. Do not convert a failed momentum trade into an unplanned long-term investment.

## Step 14 — Maintain separate records

Use [references/valuation-record-template.md](references/valuation-record-template.md).

Maintain:

1. **Valuation record:** update only for documented fundamental or risk changes.
2. **Momentum event record:** update whenever a new catalyst creates new event levels; allow it to expire without altering valuation zones.
3. **Monitoring state:** store the last observed and last alerted zone separately, the last alert price/time, next-add level, recommendation status, user-confirmed execution status, thesis version, and cluster membership.

Before mapping any zone, join the active watchlist to ACTIVE valuation records and assert exactly one valid record per ticker. Route ETFs and indices through their declared monitoring model. If the assertion fails, create a provisional record or mark `UNVALUED`/`NO_ZONE`; never infer a zone from price alone. After writing, re-read the affected records and verify the invariant.

Permitted valuation changes include actual results/guidance, supported estimate revisions, long-term margins/growth, capital structure, competition, and justified multiple changes. “Price rose,” “price fell,” or “the chart changed” is never sufficient.

## Required output

1. **One-sentence decision:** state both signals and the exact action.
2. **Route and business grade:** V/H/T plus A/B/C/D.
3. **Latest earnings/catalyst assessment:** revenue, margins, EPS, FCF, guidance, leading indicators, and thesis classification.
4. **Core financial table:** growth, margins, FCF, ROIC, net cash/debt, dilution, and industry metrics; use “not disclosed” when needed.
5. **Price-blind scenario valuation:** bear/base/bull and at least two methods.
6. **Fixed valuation zones:** date and assumptions version.
7. **Momentum scorecard:** catalyst, revision, durability, price/volume confirmation, event levels, and status.
8. **Current-price comparison:** reveal current price only here; identify both signals.
9. **Executable plan:** target weight, buy now, pullback, no pullback, next confirmation, and separate invalidation rules.
10. **Two change logs:** valuation changes and momentum-event changes; explicitly state unchanged when appropriate.

For watchlist scans, replace the full single-stock format with a concise top-five table plus: breadth statistics, the portfolio deployment budget, correlated-exposure cap, and an audit line stating which names were excluded and why. A newly triggered A/B buy zone must include a non-zero tranche unless an explicit exception above applies.

## Final checks

Verify:

- Intrinsic value was calculated before viewing current price.
- Valuation zones did not float with price.
- A narrative stock was not rejected solely because conventional P/E is high.
- A price surge was not chased without a verified forward catalyst.
- A chase-qualified event received a meaningful but controlled initial tranche.
- Value and momentum sleeves have different invalidation rules.
- The no-pullback path can actually complete the intended position.
- Current data is sourced and time-stamped.
- Prior versus new assumptions are reconciled.
- No A/B company in a valid buy zone was assigned 0% merely because earnings, volatility, or a better price might occur.
- Same-zone next-add crossings and portfolio-wide clusters were tested before declaring “no update.”
- Recommended trades were not silently treated as executed holdings.

Revise the answer if any check fails.
