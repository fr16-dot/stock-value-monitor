# Source Policy

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer sources in this order when available:

1. regulator filings and official issuer filings;
2. company investor-relations releases, presentations, and official guidance;
3. earnings-call materials and transcripts from reliable providers;
4. high-quality structured market/financial data;
5. reputable financial/news reporting;
6. analyst research as a cross-check;
7. social media, forums, and aggregators as leads only.

Social posts, rumors, screenshots, and commentary may justify investigation but must not independently change intrinsic value.

## Freshness

Use the newest applicable primary-source evidence for a deep review. Record the source date or reporting period when material.

Quote freshness is separate from fundamental-source freshness. A current filing does not validate a stale quote, and a live quote does not validate a stale valuation thesis.

## Consensus and third-party fair values

Use consensus estimates to measure surprise or as an external cross-check after the price-blind forecast is locked.

Do not use analyst target-price averages, Morningstar fair value, option-implied levels, or market multiples as the starting anchor for intrinsic value.

## Conflicting evidence

When sources conflict, preserve the conflict in review notes and prefer primary documents over secondary interpretation. Do not silently resolve ambiguity in favor of a desired valuation outcome.
