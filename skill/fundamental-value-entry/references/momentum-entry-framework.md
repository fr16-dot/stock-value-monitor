# Catalyst and Momentum Entry Framework

Read this reference for earnings gaps, breakout/chase requests, and hybrid/reflexive stocks.

## 1. Separate the event from the story

Accept events supported by primary evidence:

- Earnings acceleration plus a forward guidance increase.
- Large contract, backlog, RPO, TCV, paid-customer, capacity, or delivery inflection.
- Regulatory approval or commercial launch that changes probability-weighted economics.
- Margin or FCF inflection that validates operating leverage.
- Balance-sheet repair that removes a material survival or dilution risk.

Reject price-only explanations, recycled announcements, unsourced rumors, vague partnerships, and option activity without a business catalyst.

## 2. Measure the revision

Compare the event with all three baselines:

1. Prior company guidance.
2. Consensus expectations immediately before the event.
3. The assumptions in the existing valuation record.

Quantify revenue, EPS/FCF, margin, backlog, or milestone changes. Distinguish a one-quarter beat from a durable change in slope or duration.

## 3. Judge durability

Look for at least two:

- Sequential and year-over-year acceleration.
- Raised next-quarter and full-year guidance.
- Multi-quarter leading indicators.
- High incremental margin or cash conversion.
- Contracted demand or observable usage.
- A new product/business crossing from optionality into measurable adoption.
- Multiple estimate revisions likely rather than a single catch-up.

Apply a penalty for customer concentration, financing dependence, aggressive adjustments, unusual taxes, stock compensation, low-quality TCV, or nonbinding demand.

## 4. Confirm the tape

Record exact, time-stamped facts. Prefer:

- Relative volume of at least roughly 1.5 times normal.
- Price holding above the event open or gap midpoint.
- A close in the upper half of the event-day range.
- Sector and market outperformance.
- Follow-through or a low-volume pullback that preserves the gap.

Do not require every item. For intraday requests, label the close as pending and use smaller initial size.

Do not reject a 20%–30% gap merely because it is large. Reduce size when distance to the event-failure level makes position-level risk unacceptable.

## 5. Define event levels

- **Event low:** clearest hard failure reference; normally use a closing violation unless explicitly day trading.
- **Event open:** first test of whether institutions continue supporting the repricing.
- **Gap midpoint:** useful for controlled pullbacks; never call it intrinsic value.
- **Event high:** right-side confirmation level for a no-pullback add.
- **Prior close/full gap fill:** severe momentum failure when the catalyst was supposed to reset expectations.
- **VWAP:** optional execution aid only when reliable live data is available.

Event levels expire after the catalyst is absorbed, normally within ten trading sessions or when a newer material event occurs. Retain them longer only when they remain widely observed and fundamentally relevant.

## 6. Size by position-level risk

Estimate:

`portfolio risk = portfolio weight purchased × percentage distance to failure level`

Example: a 0.6% portfolio purchase with a 12% event-stop distance risks roughly 0.07% of portfolio value before slippage.

Default chase sizing:

| Quality/risk | Initial chase | Maximum momentum sleeve |
|---|---:|---:|
| A/B, strong verified event | 20%–30% of target | 50%–60% of target |
| C, strong but fragile event | 10%–15% | 30%–40% |
| D, verified but speculative event | 0%–5% | 10%–15% |
| T, tape-only/no verified event | 0% | 0% |

Reduce these sizes for wide stops, leverage, dilution, illiquidity, binary regulation, or portfolio correlation.

## 7. Add without demanding a pullback

Use one or more:

- Add 15%–25% after a strong event-day close and next-session hold.
- Add 15%–25% on a low-volume pullback that holds the event open or gap midpoint.
- Add 10%–20% on a new closing high with continued estimate revisions.
- Add after the next report or milestone confirms the new slope.

If price exceeds the overvalued zone, cap the momentum sleeve rather than raising fair value. Do not complete an oversized position only because the chart remains strong.

## 8. Exit the correct sleeve

Exit or reduce the momentum sleeve when:

- The stock decisively closes below the event low or fully fills a thesis-reset gap.
- Guidance or analyst estimates reverse.
- The apparent catalyst proves one-time, accounting-driven, nonbinding, or already priced.
- Volume shows distribution and follow-through fails.
- Financing, dilution, regulation, or execution changes expected payoff.

Do not automatically sell the value sleeve on a momentum failure when intrinsic-value assumptions remain intact. Do not retain the momentum sleeve solely because the long-term story is attractive.

## 9. Special handling for narrative/optionality stocks

For PLTR-style hypergrowth software, emphasize growth acceleration, U.S./core commercial demand, TCV/RPO quality, incremental margin, FCF, dilution, and estimate revisions.

For TSLA-style platform optionality, separate the existing operating business from autonomous driving, robotaxi, energy, robotics, and other options. Require measurable evidence such as paid usage, regulatory scope, interventions, unit economics, capacity, or deliveries. Do not chase rumor-only demonstrations.

For capital-intensive AI infrastructure, emphasize contracted versus nonbinding demand, financing, capex, utilization, customer concentration, power availability, and dilution.

## 10. Mandatory language

State conclusions in this form:

> Valuation signal: [zone]. Momentum signal: [status]. Action: buy [x%] of target now through the [value/momentum] sleeve; add [y%] on [specific confirmation]; reduce/exit at [specific fundamental or event failure].

Never collapse “valuation expensive” into “cannot buy,” and never collapse “price is rising” into “must chase.”
